How We Work

From Brief to Production

The same four stages, every engagement — whether it is a six-week build or a year of platform work. Each one ends in something written down, so you always know what was decided and why.

Discovery

We map what you are actually trying to change before anyone estimates it: the people who will use it, the systems it has to live alongside, and the constraints nobody thought to write down. It ends in a written estimate you can hold us to.

Blueprinting

Architecture, data model and interface settled on paper, with the trade-off behind each choice recorded next to it. Disagreeing at this stage costs a conversation; disagreeing later costs a rebuild. You get a blueprint and a dated delivery plan.

Delivery

Two-week cycles, each ending in a deployed build you can open yourself. You see progress in working software rather than in a status report, which is also how a change in scope gets caught while it is still cheap.

Operate

Launch is the start of the expensive half. Alerting, security patching and a named contact on agreed response times — because most software fails slowly, and slowly is the failure you can still do something about.

Engagement Models

Four Ways This Starts

Most work arrives in one of these shapes. If yours does not, say so — the model should fit the problem, not the other way round.

Fixed Scope, Fixed Price

A Defined Build With a Date You Can Put in a Plan

For work whose shape is already clear. We blueprint it, quote it once, and carry the risk of our own estimate — the figure you approve is the figure you pay, and a change of scope is a conversation before it is an invoice.

An Embedded Squad

Engineers Who Join Your Standups, Not Just Your Inbox

When the direction is yours and what you need is capacity behind it. Our people work inside your process, on your board and under your review — and everything they write is yours, documented well enough that your team can carry it on alone.

Modernisation in Slices

Replacing a Legacy System While It Keeps Serving Customers

Nobody can afford a cutover weekend any more. We open a seam through the existing system and move it across one capability at a time, so every step is small enough to reverse and the old platform is never switched off before the new one has earned it.

A Second Opinion First

Two Weeks of Audit Before You Commit to Anything

For teams who cannot yet tell whether the problem is the architecture, the process or the roadmap. You get the findings and an honest recommendation — including the one where the right answer is your current team and a smaller change than expected.
